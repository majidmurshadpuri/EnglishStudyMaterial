Lesson 1: Meet the Critic – The Life of Samuel Johnson

Before we look at what Samuel Johnson wrote about Shakespeare, we need to understand who Samuel Johnson was. In literary studies, knowing the background of the critic helps us understand why they value certain things over others.

What is Literary Criticism?

Before we dive in, let’s define our main subject. Literary Criticism is the practice of studying, evaluating, and interpreting works of literature. Think of a critic as a very careful reader who explains why a book is good, why it might be flawed, and what it tells us about life [1, 2].

1. Early Life and Physical Struggles

Samuel Johnson was born in 1709 in Lichfield, England [3]. His father was a bookseller, which meant Samuel grew up surrounded by books—the perfect environment for a future critic [3].

However, his life was not easy. As a baby, he caught a disease called tuberculosis from his nurse, which left him with very poor eyesight and made him partially deaf [3]. Later, smallpox left his face scarred [3].

Why does this matter? Despite these physical challenges, Johnson was incredibly determined. He couldn't play sports, so he taught himself to box and swim, and he worked harder than anyone else at his studies [4]. This tells us that Johnson was a man of immense willpower—a trait that shows up later in his writing.

2. The Oxford Incident and Independence

Johnson went to Oxford University, but he had to leave after only thirteen months because he ran out of money [4]. This was a very painful time for him.

There is a famous story from this time that teaches us about his character. Johnson was so poor that his shoes had holes in them, and his toes were peeking out [5]. One night, a kind person left a new pair of shoes outside his door. Instead of being grateful, Johnson was angry and threw them away [5]. He was "fierce" about his independence and did not want anyone’s charity [4, 5]. He wanted to earn his way through his own hard work [4].

3. Struggle and Success in London

After leaving university, Johnson suffered from deep depression [5]. He eventually married Elizabeth Porter (who was twenty years older than him) and moved to London to try to make a living as a writer [6, 7].

In London, he wrote everything: poems, essays, reviews, and even a play called Irene [7]. He was slowly building a reputation as a brilliant thinker. One of his most important early achievements was creating a Critical Biography [8].

Technical Term – Critical Biography: This is a type of book that tells the story of a person’s life while also providing a deep analysis and evaluation of the work they created [8].

4. The "Dictionary" – A Massive Achievement

Before he finished his work on Shakespeare, Johnson did something almost impossible. He decided to write a Dictionary of the English Language [8].

To give you an idea of how hard this was: in France, it took 40 scholars 55 years to finish a similar dictionary [9]. Johnson planned to do it in three years, and although it actually took him eight, he did most of it by himself [9]. When it was published in 1755, it made him famous [9].

Because he was so successful, the later part of the 18th century is often called the "Age of Johnson" [10]. He was the most influential literary figure of his time [10].

Wait, What is a "Preface"?

Our main text is called the "Preface to Shakespeare." Before we go further, let's define that term.

Technical Term – Preface: An introduction at the beginning of a book that explains its purpose, the methods the author used, and often gives some background information on the subject.

In 1765, after years of delay, Johnson published his own edition of Shakespeare’s plays [11, 12]. The Preface was the 72-page introduction he wrote for that edition [13]. It is now considered one of the greatest pieces of Neo-classical criticism [13].

We will define "Neo-classical" in our next lesson, as it is a big concept that explains how Johnson thought about art.
