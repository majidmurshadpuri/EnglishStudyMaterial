1. What is Classicism?

Basic Idea – Start Here

Imagine you are a student in a classroom. Your teacher says: “Write a poem, but follow these rules – keep it balanced, use clear language, teach a moral lesson, and look at how the great writers of ancient Greece and Rome wrote.”

That, in simple words, is the spirit of Classicism.

Classicism is a literary movement and style that looks back to the literature of ancient Greece and Rome (especially Homer, Virgil, Horace, and Aristotle) and tries to follow their ideals of order, reason, balance, clarity, and moral purpose.

Simple Definition for Exam

Classicism is the literary approach that values reason over emotion, order over chaos, rules over free expression, and universal human nature over individual feelings. It believes that literature should both delight and instruct.

Key phrase to remember: “Art is the imitation of nature according to fixed rules derived from the ancients.”

Classicism vs Neo-Classicism

In English literature, when we say “Classicism,” we mostly mean Neo-Classicism – the revival of classical ideals in the period roughly from 1660 to 1798.

  • Classical = original Greek and Roman literature
  • Neo-Classical = the new classicism of 17th–18th century England and Europe that imitated those models

In exam answers, both terms are often used interchangeably for the English literature of Dryden, Pope, and Johnson.

Exam Writing Tip

Always begin your answer with a clear definition in simple language, then expand. Examiners love when a student shows understanding first, then details.

2. Historical Background

Why Did Classicism Rise in England?

After the English Civil War and the Puritan rule under Oliver Cromwell, the monarchy was restored in 1660 (Restoration of Charles II). Society wanted stability, order, and polish after years of chaos.

People were tired of extreme religious emotion and political turbulence. They turned to reason, good manners, and the orderly models of ancient Rome.

Time Period
  • 1660 – 1700: Restoration Period (Dryden dominant)
  • 1700 – 1745: Augustan Age (Pope, Swift, Addison)
  • 1745 – 1798: Age of Johnson (transition towards Romanticism)

The period ends around 1798 when Wordsworth and Coleridge published Lyrical Ballads, which started Romanticism.

Influence of French Classicism

England was heavily influenced by French writers like Boileau, who wrote strict rules for poetry and drama. French classical theatre (Corneille, Racine) followed the Three Unities strictly. English writers adopted many of these ideas, though they were never as rigid as the French.

The Spirit of the Age

This was also the age of the Enlightenment or the Age of Reason. Science (Newton), philosophy (Locke), and coffee-house culture encouraged clear thinking, wit, and social observation. Literature became a tool for correcting society through satire and moral teaching.

3. Core Features of Classicism

1. Reason and Order

The classical writer believes the universe is orderly. Literature must reflect that order. Emotions are controlled; reason guides the poet. Wild imagination is dangerous.

Example: Pope’s poetry is always balanced, logical, and carefully structured.
2. Imitation of the Ancients

Writers studied Homer, Virgil, Horace, and Aristotle as perfect models. “Imitation” does not mean copying word for word; it means following their principles of structure, style, and moral purpose.

3. Decorum

Decorum means “proper behaviour” in literature. The style must suit the subject. A king should speak in elevated language; a common man in simple language. Tragedy and comedy should not mix freely.

4. Clarity, Precision, and Restraint

Language must be clear, exact, and polished. No obscure metaphors or emotional outbursts. The ideal is “correctness.”

5. Universal Human Nature

Classical writers believe that human nature is the same in all ages. Therefore, literature should deal with general truths, not personal, private feelings.

“The proper study of mankind is man.” – Alexander Pope
6. Moral Purpose – Delight and Instruct

Literature must please the reader and teach a moral lesson. Horace’s famous idea: dulce et utile (sweet and useful).

7. Wit and Satire

Wit (clever, intellectual humour) and satire (criticism of human folly) became major tools. Writers wanted to correct society by laughing at its faults.

8. Emphasis on Form and Rules

The heroic couplet, the three unities in drama, fixed genres – all show love for form. The poem or play must have a clear beginning, middle, and end.

4. Important Rules & Critical Principles

The Three Unities (for Drama)

Taken mainly from Aristotle (though Aristotle mentioned only Unity of Action clearly) and developed by French critics:

  • Unity of Action – one main plot, no sub-plots
  • Unity of Time – the action should happen within 24 hours
  • Unity of Place – the action should happen in one location

English writers (except some strict ones) followed these less rigidly than the French.

Poetic Justice

Good characters should be rewarded and bad characters punished at the end. This satisfies the moral sense of the audience.

Verisimilitude (Probability)

Events in the play or poem must seem believable and possible according to reason and common sense.

Genre Hierarchy

Tragedy and epic were considered higher forms. Comedy and satire were lower but still important. Mixing genres was generally disliked.

The Heroic Couplet

The favourite poetic form of the age. Two rhyming lines of iambic pentameter. Dryden and Pope perfected it. It forces clarity and balance.

“True wit is Nature to advantage dressed,
What oft was thought, but ne’er so well expressed.” – Pope

5. Major Writers of the Classical Age

John Dryden (1631–1700)

Father of English Criticism Poet Laureate

Bridge between Restoration and full Neo-Classicism. Master of satire, heroic play, and critical essays. His Essay of Dramatic Poesy is the foundation of English literary criticism.

Alexander Pope (1688–1744)

Perfect Classicist Heroic Couplet Master

The greatest poetic voice of the Augustan Age. His works show perfect control, wit, and moral concern. Key works: Essay on Criticism, The Rape of the Lock, Essay on Man.

Jonathan Swift (1667–1745)

Greatest Satirist

Used irony and satire to attack human pride and folly. Gulliver’s Travels and A Modest Proposal are masterpieces of classical satire.

Joseph Addison & Richard Steele

Periodical Essay

Through The Spectator and The Tatler, they taught middle-class readers good manners, morality, and taste in polished, clear prose.

Dr. Samuel Johnson (1709–1784)

Age of Johnson Dictionary

Last major classical figure. His criticism is practical and moral. He admired Shakespeare but judged him by classical standards of correctness and morality. Famous for Lives of the Poets and Preface to Shakespeare.

6. Aristotle’s Influence on Classicism

Why Aristotle Matters

Aristotle’s Poetics (written around 335 BCE) is the single most important critical text for classical and neo-classical writers. Almost every rule of Classicism can be traced back to him (or to Horace, who also followed him).

Key Ideas from Aristotle Used by Classicists
  • Mimesis – Literature is an imitation of life / nature
  • Catharsis – Tragedy purifies emotions of pity and fear
  • Unity of Action – one complete and whole action
  • Plot is more important than character
  • Tragedy should deal with serious actions of noble persons
  • Poetry is more philosophical than history (it deals with the universal)
How English Classicists Used Aristotle

Dryden discussed Aristotle in his critical essays. Pope referred to him with great respect. Dr. Johnson used Aristotelian ideas while judging Shakespeare. The idea that literature should follow “rules” and aim at moral improvement comes largely from this tradition.

In the syllabus, Aristotle is listed among the important critics. Any answer on Classicism must mention his influence.

7. Dryden, Pope & Dr. Johnson in Detail

John Dryden – The Pioneer

Dryden made classical principles popular in England. In An Essay of Dramatic Poesy (1668), he defends English drama while still respecting classical rules. He shows that rules should serve the writer, not enslave him.

His satires (Absalom and Achitophel, Mac Flecknoe) use classical forms for contemporary political and literary attack.

Alexander Pope – The Perfect Expression

Pope’s entire poetic career is a demonstration of classical ideals:

  • Essay on Criticism – teaches the rules of good writing and judgment
  • The Rape of the Lock – a mock-epic that uses classical epic machinery for a trivial modern incident
  • Essay on Man – philosophical poem teaching acceptance of the divine order

He believed that Nature (human nature and the order of the universe) is the best guide, and the ancients had already discovered the best ways to express it.

Dr. Samuel Johnson – The Moral Critic

Johnson represents the mature and somewhat flexible phase of Classicism. He valued reason, morality, and general nature, but he also praised Shakespeare’s genius even when Shakespeare broke classical rules.

In his Preface to Shakespeare, Johnson defends Shakespeare against strict neo-classical attacks, yet still judges him by standards of moral usefulness and probability.

Johnson said literature should help us better endure life or enjoy it. This is pure classical moral purpose.

8. Classicism vs Romanticism

This is one of the most expected comparison questions in the exam. Understand the contrast deeply.

Aspect Classicism Romanticism
Guiding Force Reason & Rules Emotion & Imagination
Source of Poetry Universal human nature Individual feelings & personal experience
Attitude to Nature Nature as ordered system Nature as living, spiritual presence
Language Polished, elevated, clear Simple, natural, rustic (Wordsworth)
Form Strict forms (heroic couplet) Freedom of form
Purpose Delight + Instruct (moral) Express emotion & imagination
Ideal Balance, restraint, decorum Spontaneity, intensity, originality
Models Greece & Rome Medieval, folk, nature, self

Remember: Romanticism was a reaction against the restrictions of Classicism. Wordsworth and Coleridge deliberately rejected the “rules” and artificial diction of the previous age.

9. Literary Forms Favourite of Classicists

Mock Epic / Mock Heroic

A comic form that uses the grand style of classical epic (gods, battles, elevated language) for a trivial subject. The Rape of the Lock by Pope is the supreme example. It shows classical technique used for satire.

Verse Essay / Didactic Poetry

Poems that teach. Pope’s Essay on Criticism and Essay on Man are perfect examples. They combine poetry with moral and critical instruction.

Satire

The dominant mode. Personal satire (against individuals) and general satire (against human vices). Dryden, Pope, and Swift are masters.

Heroic Drama

Popular in the Restoration. Written in heroic couplets, dealing with love and honour of noble characters. Dryden wrote several.

Periodical Essay

Short prose pieces published in journals. Addison and Steele used them to spread classical ideals of taste, manners, and morality among the rising middle class.

10. Expected Descriptive Questions & Model Approach

Q1. What is Classicism? Discuss its main features.

How to answer (structure):

  1. Clear definition (2–3 lines)
  2. Historical context (brief)
  3. List 6–8 main features with short explanation and one example each
  4. Conclude with its importance or legacy

Model Opening

“Classicism in English literature refers to the literary movement of the late 17th and 18th centuries that sought to revive the ideals of ancient Greek and Roman literature — order, reason, restraint, clarity, and moral purpose. It dominated English writing from the Restoration (1660) to the end of the Age of Johnson.”

Q2. Discuss the contribution of Dryden / Pope / Johnson to Classicism.

For each writer: (a) place in the movement, (b) major works, (c) how the works show classical principles, (d) critical contribution.

Q3. Examine the influence of Aristotle on Neo-Classical criticism.

Explain mimesis, unities, catharsis, moral function of literature, and how Dryden, Pope, and Johnson used these ideas.

Q4. Compare Classicism and Romanticism.

Use a clear table or point-by-point contrast under headings: reason vs emotion, form, language, subject matter, purpose, attitude to nature and society.

Q5. Write a note on the Heroic Couplet / Mock Epic / Decorum.

Define the term, give origin, show how classicists used it, give 1–2 examples, and explain its significance.

Q6. “The classical age is the age of reason and order.” Discuss.

Connect the literary features with the larger intellectual climate of the Enlightenment and Restoration society.
